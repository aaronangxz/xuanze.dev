## What it is

<Draft>
Planning an event means juggling a design tool for the invitation, a spreadsheet for guests, a chat group for RSVPs and another spreadsheet for the budget. Avenly puts all of it behind one event: hosts design a public invitation page, and everything after that, from RSVPs to the seating chart to who has checked in at the door, hangs off the same guest list.
</Draft>

Guests reach their invitation, RSVP, entry pass and the photo wall through a magic link. There are no guest accounts and no passwords to forget.

## What's in it

- **Invitation pages** that hosts design and publish, with RSVP built in.
- **Guest management**: invitation groups, RSVP overrides, an activity log, and reminders over SMS, WhatsApp and email.
- **Seating, budgets and gifts**: a seating chart with tables and aisles, budget categories and payments, and cash and gift recording per group.
- **Event day**: QR check-in with entry passes and arrival trends, shareable schedules and itineraries.
- **Moments and Gallery**: a live photo wall that guests upload to during the event, and a post-event gallery with albums, tagging and curated views.
- **Collaborators** with fine-grained permissions, and Free and Pro plans on Stripe.

## How it's built

The backend is Go, serving gRPC with an HTTP gateway in front of it, so one proto file is the contract for roughly 150 RPCs and both the Go and TypeScript types are generated from it. Queries are plain SQL compiled to type-safe Go with sqlc, on PostgreSQL. The frontend is Next.js and React. The API runs on Railway and the frontend on Vercel.

## Making sure it doesn't break

<Draft>
Every RPC has to be declared public or protected in the auth interceptor. A test walks the whole service definition and fails the build if a new RPC is in neither list, so an endpoint can't ship unauthenticated by accident. Plan limits live in one table on the server and are served to the app, so a price or a limit is never hard-coded in two places. Stripe webhooks are de-duplicated by event ID, because Stripe does deliver the same event twice.
</Draft>
